### Background

Greg is 46 and a field service technician. He bought the house on Quarry Hill Road in 2019. In early 2024, after a long gap between jobs, he fell behind on several debts and filed a Chapter 13 case (No. 24-10382) in the fictional United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Brandywine. The court confirmed his repayment plan in May 2024. He kept his mortgage payments current throughout, but after a second job change in 2026 he missed plan payments to the trustee. The trustee moved to dismiss, and the court **dismissed the case on July 14, 2026, without a discharge**.

In September he applies to refinance a car loan and pulls his credit report. Harbor Point's mortgage tradeline shows the account as *discharged in bankruptcy*, which is wrong: there was no discharge, and he has never stopped paying the mortgage. He asks the lawyer who handled his bankruptcy to sort it out, and signs an authorization for her to deal with Harbor Point on his behalf.

## What a good outcome looks like to Monica

Written confirmation, within days, that the dispute and the documents were received, that her client will not be contacted directly, who is reviewing it and when she will hear back. What she must **not** get is a letter that pre-judges the investigation: saying the reporting "has been corrected", or anything about her client's liability, before Compliance has done its review. Any letter to Greg's own address would be a serious error.
